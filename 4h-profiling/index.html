How this archive is laid out

The three documents overlap and build on each other, so they are kept as three modules in the order they were taught. Within every lesson the structure is always the same, so you always know where to look.

The shape of every lesson

1 · Rule set — the mechanical, checkable conditions, sitting above the diagram. These are the framework's own rules, phrased so you can tick them off against a live chart.

2 · Plate — the original diagram from the source document, framed and enlargeable. Click any plate to open it full-screen.

3 · Reading — the explanation directly beneath the plate: what price is actually doing, why the rule exists, and what the diagram is showing you.

4 · How to approach it — the sequence to follow at the chart, then what invalidates the idea.

Two things to keep straight

Indices and forex run on different clocks. The session windows are not the same for the two instrument families, and the source documents state them separately. Wherever a time appears in this archive it is labelled with the instrument it belongs to. Never carry a time across from one to the other.

Times are New York time. The source documents give clock times without ever naming a timezone, but the sessions they describe — the 8:30 and 9:30 driver times, the 12:00 lunch, the NY open — only line up under New York time. That is the reading used throughout; it is an inference from context, not a statement the documents make.

What this is, and what it is not

This is a study reference — an organised restatement of what the source documents say, with the mechanisms explained. It is not financial advice, not a recommendation, and not a claim that this framework works. Any performance figure quoted here (such as the 8–12% win-rate improvement attributed to SMT divergence) is the original author's claim, reproduced as such, and has not been verified.

Where the source documents are ambiguous, silent, or internally inconsistent, this archive says so rather than papering over the gap.
