State

four treatments · nothing here is saved

The check-in is the same in all four — one pad, four corners, four facts, one optional line. The corners, the block on the gate, the lock once you have traded and the history coloured by what the ledger did are unchanged. Only the arrangement differs. Every one below is live: place a dot, tick things, resize the window.

A

One column

no headings, no split

The two cards collapse into one. The pad leads, the sentence under it tells you where you landed, the four facts become a row you scan in one pass, and the note and the button share the last line. Nothing is titled, because a pad with four named corners does not need a label telling you it is a pad.

a green day a red day nothing traded

B

The pad is the screen

no card at all

No border, no panel, no button that looks like a button. The pad is large and sits on the glass directly; the facts are four quiet marks under it and the confirm is a line of type. The least form-like of the four — it reads as a thing you touch once rather than something you fill in.

C

The dial

round, like the backtesting ring

The same two axes bent into a circle. The centre is the middle of everything and the rim is the extreme, so how far out you place the dot is how strongly you feel it — a square gives you no way to say “a bit tilted”. It also rhymes with the ring on the backtesting screen, which is the only other round object in the app.

D

The band

one row, above everything else

The whole check-in as a single strip you could put at the top of any screen. Small pad, four ticks, done. It gives up the history behind you — the pad is too small to read it, and the outer labels with it — only the four corners survive, and the line beside the pad says where you landed. Fastest to answer, least to look at.
