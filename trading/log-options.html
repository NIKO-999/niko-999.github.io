The four figures

four ways to hide them · nothing here is saved

Win rate, average R, profit factor and expectancy, on the log. All four treatments below start shut and none of them is a card. Open one and you get the same thing every time: one line, one typeface, tabular figures, no colour on any of the four — a red profit factor is the reason they left the metrics screen in the first place. Whichever you pick remembers whether it was open, like every other folding panel in the app.

A

The hairline

shut, it is a divider

No title, no word, nothing sitting in space. Shut it is a rule between the head and the filters — something you would read as spacing rather than as a control. The only tell is a small caret on the line, at a third opacity until you come near it. The quietest of the four, and the easiest to never find again.

B

One word

“figures”, at half opacity

One word in the smallest type the app owns, greyed to half, sitting on the right where nothing else is. It is findable without being addressed to you. Open, the four sit under it right-aligned, so the whole thing stays in that corner and never crosses the page.

C

Behind the count

nothing in the page at all

The log's head already says how many trades there are. Make that line the control: click it and it becomes the four figures, click again and it is the count. Nothing is ever added to the page — no rule, no word, no row — and the four occupy a slot that was already spent. The most hidden of the four, and the only one with nothing new on screen.

D

Bare digits

there, but unlabelled

Shut is not empty here — it is the four numbers with every word stripped off, at half opacity in the corner. You can glance at them without being told what they mean, which is the difference between checking and being judged. Click and the labels and the sample caveat appear. The only one that shows you anything before you ask.

Round two not a panel at all

A to D are one idea at four volumes: press a thing, four figures appear. These four each do something a folding panel cannot — the numbers live in a shape, in a rule that was already on the page, in a sentence, or in the margin. All still open to the same plain line if you want the actual figures.

E

The signature

a shape, not a score

Four arms from one point, each as long as one of the figures, drawn at 22 pixels beside the trade count. You cannot read a verdict off it — there is no number and no scale — only whether it is fatter or thinner than the one you remember. Which is the only comparison that was ever any use. Click it for the figures themselves.

F

The rule is the win rate

no new ink on the page

There is already a hairline between the head and the filters. Make part of it brighter, and make that part the fraction of trades that work — so the divider you were ignoring is the figure. Nothing is added: the ink that was there means something now. You would not know unless someone told you, and after that you cannot unsee it.

G

A sentence

the figures, written out

Prose cannot be a scoreboard. There is no cell to go red, nothing ranked against anything, and no percentage sitting on its own looking like a mark out of a hundred — the same four facts, said in the app's voice, in one line you read rather than check. Click it and the raw figures appear underneath for when you actually want them.

H

The margin

off the page, into the edge

Four short strokes down the right-hand margin, each as long as one figure, at a third opacity. It is not in the reading column at all, so it never interrupts a note — you see it the way you see the length of a scrollbar, which is the only way a number gets to sit in front of you all day without becoming a verdict. Click the margin for the figures.
